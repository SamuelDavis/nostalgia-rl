# Zones and terrain

How other games shape terrain from a few noise parameters, place bespoke finite generators in an infinite world, and attach separate spaces to it, judged against our constraints (infinite, chunked, bounded neighbourhood, exact float ops, peer-to-peer). Ends with options for a "deep forest". Researched 2026-10-16. Items marked **unverified** could not be traced to a primary source.

## Verdict

- Minecraft's terrain is three 2D noises (continentalness, erosion, folded ridges) fed through nested cubic splines into a height offset. Everything except the `factor` × 3D-noise term reduces to a 2D heightmap, and splines need only `+ − × ÷`, so the approach fits.
- Biomes in Minecraft are boxes in the same parameter space that shapes terrain. "Woodland vs deep forest" (humidity bands) and "foothills vs high mountain" (peaks-and-valleys bands at low erosion) are bands of one parameter there.
- Bespoke generators fit an infinite world when each instance has a hashed anchor on a coarse grid and a hard maximum extent. A chunk then consults only anchors within that extent, which is what Minecraft structures do. Cataclysm: DDA is the counterexample: its overmap edges depend on generation order.
- For a deep forest, the best fit is option (b): bespoke generation per macro cell, with crossing points on each cell edge taken from a hash of the shared edge. See the [recommendation](#recommendation).

## 1. Minecraft overworld terrain (1.18+)

Data below is the vanilla data pack as mirrored by [misode/mcmeta `data` branch](https://github.com/misode/mcmeta/tree/data/data/minecraft/worldgen), version [26.4-snapshot-4](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/misode/mcmeta/data/version.json). Field names were renamed in 26.3 snapshots (`firstOctave`→`base_octave`, `amplitudes`→`amplitude_modifiers`, [Noise](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Noise)). I did not diff the spline values against 1.18 (**unverified** that they are unchanged).

### Composition

From the density-function files ([offset](https://github.com/misode/mcmeta/blob/data/data/minecraft/worldgen/density_function/overworld/offset.json), [depth](https://github.com/misode/mcmeta/blob/data/data/minecraft/worldgen/density_function/overworld/depth.json), [sloped_cheese](https://github.com/misode/mcmeta/blob/data/data/minecraft/worldgen/density_function/overworld/sloped_cheese.json), [ridges_folded](https://github.com/misode/mcmeta/blob/data/data/minecraft/worldgen/density_function/overworld/ridges_folded.json)):

```
pv            = −3·(| |ridges| − 2/3 | − 1/3)      = 1 − |3|ridges| − 2|   ("peaks and valleys")
offset        = −0.50375 + spline(continents → erosion → pv [→ ridges])
depth         = lerp_y(−64: 1.5 … 320: −1.5) + offset
jag           = jaggedness_spline(continents, erosion, pv, ridges) · half_negative(noise jagged, xz_scale 1500)
sloped_cheese = 4·quarter_negative((depth + jag) · factor) + base_3d_noise
```

`final_density` then applies `interpolated` (cells 4×8 blocks), `squeeze` and cave carving ([final_density](https://github.com/misode/mcmeta/blob/data/data/minecraft/worldgen/density_function/overworld/final_density.json)). Solid where the density is above 0 ([Noise router](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Noise_router)). The `pv` formula matches the wiki's ([World generation](https://minecraft.wiki/w/World_generation)).

**Derived (my arithmetic):** ignoring the 3D term, the surface is where `depth + jag = 0`, i.e. `y ≈ 128·(1.5 + offset + jag) − 64`. With spline output 0 that gives y ≈ 63.5, vanilla sea level 63 ([overworld.json](https://github.com/misode/mcmeta/blob/data/data/minecraft/worldgen/noise_settings/overworld.json)). So one unit of spline output is 128 blocks, and the splines are a heightmap in disguise.

### Noises

From the [noise files](https://github.com/misode/mcmeta/tree/data/data/minecraft/worldgen/noise). The router samples continentalness, erosion and ridge with `xz_scale 0.25` and `y_scale 0` (2D), warped by `shift_x`/`shift_z` ([continents](https://github.com/misode/mcmeta/blob/data/data/minecraft/worldgen/density_function/overworld/continents.json)). `shift_*` samples a noise at x/4 and multiplies by 4 ([Density function](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Density_function)).

| Noise           | base_octave  | amplitude_modifiers  | Wavelengths with non-zero weight (blocks)\* |
| --------------- | ------------ | -------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| continentalness | −9           | 1,1,2,2,2,1,1,1,1    | 2048 … 8                                    |
| erosion         | −9           | 1,1,0,1,1            | 2048, 1024, 256, 128                        |
| ridge           | −7           | 1,2,1,0,0,0          | 512, 256, 128                               |
| temperature     | −10          | 1.5,0,1,0,0,0        | 4096, 1024                                  |
| vegetation      | −8           | 1,1,0,0,0,0          | 1024, 512                                   |
| jagged          | −16 (16 oct) | all 1, xz_scale 1500 | ≈ 44 and finer                              |

\*Assumes octave _k_ has frequency 2^_k_ per unit input. That is what the decompiled `PerlinNoise` does, but the wiki doesn't state it (**unverified** from a fetched source). Wavelength = 2^−k / xz_scale.

### What makes which landform

Read off the offset spline (branch `continents ≥ 0.25`, values are `offset` at pv = −1 / 0 / +1):

| Landform             | Mechanism in the data                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 | 2D?                                                                  |
| -------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Rolling plains/hills | High erosion (0.2–0.7): offset −0.1 → 0.01 → 0.1, small slope in pv.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  | Yes                                                                  |
| Mountains            | Erosion −0.85: offset rises linearly with pv, 0.20 → 0.72 → 1.23 (continents 0.25), up to 1.5 at continents 1.0 (≈ y 255).                                                                                                                                                                                                                            | Yes                                                                  |
| Plateaus             | Erosion −0.35: offset −0.25 at pv −1 (derivative 0.5), then **flat 0.35 from pv −0.4 to 0.4 with derivative 0** (0.5 at continents 1.0). A zero-derivative run gives the flat top.                                                                                                                                                                    | Yes                                                                  |
| Cliffs               | Closely spaced spline locations with very different values: e.g. at continents 1.0, pv 1, offset drops 1.0 → 0.6 between erosion −0.4 and −0.35. Horizontal steepness = spline slope × noise gradient.                                                                                                                                                | Yes, steep heightmap steps                                           |
| Jagged peaks         | `jaggedness` spline is non-zero only for continents ≥ −0.11, erosion < −0.375, pv > 0.45 (values 0.63 or 0.315, by sign of `ridges`). It multiplies high-frequency `jagged` noise, which `half_negative` biases upward. Approximate in 2D as extra height noise ∝ 1/factor |
| Sheer vertical walls | Only from the 3D term plus `squeeze`/interpolation. In 2D, a vertical wall is just a large height step between tiles.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 | Partly: our cliffs already are this                                  |

**Fit.** A 2D port is `height = level(base + spline(c, e, pv) + jaggedness·jagged)`. Cubic splines with location/value/derivative points need only `+ − × ÷`, so they are exact. The evaluation formula (Hermite, and extrapolation past the end points) is **unverified**: the wiki only says "cubic spline". Caution from phase 1: steep spline segments wider than a tile become bands of cliffs on every tile edge. Keep the ledge flooring, or make steep segments sub-tile.

### Multi-noise biome selection

- Six parameters: temperature, humidity (vegetation), continentalness, erosion, weirdness (= ridges), depth. Each biome is a set of intervals (a box) in this 6D space. A point outside every box takes the closest box ([World generation](https://minecraft.wiki/w/World_generation)). The distance metric isn't documented on the wiki (**unverified**). The router's noises "do not affect terrain shape" except through `final_density`, which reuses the same noises ([Noise router](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Noise_router)). So biome and landform correlate by construction.
- Bands ([World generation](https://minecraft.wiki/w/World_generation)):
  - continentalness: deep ocean −1.05…−0.455, ocean …−0.19, coast …−0.11, near-inland …0.03, mid-inland …0.3, far-inland …1.0.
  - erosion levels 0–6: cuts at −0.78, −0.375, −0.2225, 0.05, 0.45, 0.55.
  - pv: valleys −1…−0.85, low …−0.2, mid …0.2, high …0.7, peaks …1.0.
  - humidity levels 0–4: cuts at −0.35, −0.1, 0.1, 0.3.
- **Woodland vs deep forest:** in the "middle biomes" table, the humidity-4 row (0.3…1.0) holds Dark Forest. Lower-humidity rows hold Forest, Birch Forest and Plains. Dense vs light woodland is therefore a humidity band. Which temperature column each cell sits in was ambiguous in the fetched table (**unverified**).
- **Foothills vs high mountain:** at erosion 0–1, pv "high" gives slopes (Snowy Slopes, Grove, plateau biomes) and pv "peaks" gives Jagged, Frozen or Stony Peaks. Erosion 2 gives plateaus. These are bands of one parameter (pv) at fixed low erosion.

## 2. Bespoke finite generators in an infinite world

### Minecraft structures and jigsaw

- **Placement** (`structure_set`, `random_spread`): the world is divided into `spacing`×`spacing`-chunk regions. Each region gets one attempt, offset by a seeded random in [0, spacing − separation) (`linear`; `triangular` averages two draws). `salt` decorrelates sets, `frequency` thins attempts, and the biome at the position can veto ([Structure set](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Structure_set)). Villages use spacing 34, separation 8, salt 10387312 ([villages.json](https://github.com/misode/mcmeta/blob/data/data/minecraft/worldgen/structure_set/villages.json)). Strongholds use `concentric_rings` (count 128) instead, a global ring layout around the origin ([strongholds.json](https://github.com/misode/mcmeta/blob/data/data/minecraft/worldgen/structure_set/strongholds.json)).
- **Extent:** a jigsaw structure grows from `start_pool` to depth `size` ≤ 20. Pieces stay within `max_distance_from_center` ≤ 128 blocks (≤ 116 with terrain adaptation) ([Structure/JSON format](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Structure/JSON_format)). Village plains: size 6, max distance 80, start projected to `WORLD_SURFACE_WG`, `terrain_adaptation: beard_thin` ([village_plains.json](https://github.com/misode/mcmeta/blob/data/data/minecraft/worldgen/structure/village_plains.json)).
- **Chunk query:** the generation steps are `structure_starts` → `structure_references` → `biomes` → `terrain` → `features` …. In `structure_starts`, the chunk owning a start computes and stores the positions of all its pieces. In `structure_references`, nearby chunks store a reference to that start chunk ([World generation](https://minecraft.wiki/w/World_generation)). The search radius (8 chunks, consistent with 128 blocks) is **unverified**. `beard_*`/`bury` adapt terrain density around pieces. Features may write only into the 3×3 chunk neighbourhood ([World generation](https://minecraft.wiki/w/World_generation)).
- **Fit: excellent.** Each start is a pure function of (seed, salt, region) plus terrain sampled at a few points, and its pieces fall within a known radius. This is exactly the bounded-neighbourhood rule: our macro cell plays the role of the region, and our camps already work this way.

### Cataclysm: Dark Days Ahead overmaps

- An overmap is 180×180 overmap terrains (OMTs) ([`OMAPX`](https://github.com/CleverRaven/Cataclysm-DDA/blob/master/src/map_scale_constants.h)). An OMT is 2×2 submaps of 12×12 tiles (`SEEX = 12`, same file), so an overmap is 4320 tiles across. The overmap fixes where roads, cities and buildings go; local mapgen fills in the actual layout lazily ([OVERMAP.md](https://github.com/CleverRaven/Cataclysm-DDA/blob/master/doc/JSON/OVERMAP.md)).
- **Edges are order-dependent.** `generate()` first calls `populate_connections_out_from_neighbors`, which copies road and other exits from neighbouring overmaps that **already exist** ([overmap.cpp](https://github.com/CleverRaven/Cataclysm-DDA/blob/master/src/overmap.cpp)). New random exits ("at least 3 exit points, to guarantee road continuity") are added only toward ungenerated neighbours, and the shuffle uses the global `rng_get_engine()`. The same seed can therefore give different worlds depending on exploration order. **Not a fit**; avoid this pattern.
- **Specials:** placed in 15×15-OMT sectors by random trial. Fixed specials are rotatable multi-OMT stamps. Mutable specials are jigsaw-like: each piece declares a join per edge (and above/below), and a join "must match the opposite join for the adjacent overmap". Connections (road, sewer) are routed afterwards by `overmap_connection`, a least-cost rule set: fields preferred over forest, over swamp, over river, and a road over a river becomes a bridge ([OVERMAP.md](https://github.com/CleverRaven/Cataclysm-DDA/blob/master/doc/JSON/OVERMAP.md)). The join-matching idea fits; the sector trial loop is per-overmap and global within the overmap.

### Caves of Qud zones

- A zone is an 80×25-cell screen. A world-map cell is a parasang of 3×3 zones. Zone IDs are `World.ParasangX.Y.ZoneX.Y.Z`, e.g. `JoppaWorld.53.3.1.1.10`, with Z 10 the surface and higher Z deeper ([Modding: Zones and Worlds](https://wiki.cavesofqud.com/wiki/Modding:Intro_-_Zones_and_Worlds)).
- Zones are built by builders "applied in succession", then postbuilders, optionally from a static `.rpm` map. World-map generation attaches builders to terrain tiles, e.g. `RoadNorthMouth`/`RoadSouthMouth` at `ZoneBuilderPriority.LATE` ([Modding: Worlds](https://wiki.cavesofqud.com/wiki/Modding:Worlds)). `RiverBuilder` routes rivers by pathfinding over noise-weighted cells ([Modding: Zone Procedural Generation](https://wiki.cavesofqud.com/wiki/Modding:Zone_Procedural_Generation)). Seeds are hashed from the world seed plus a string ([Modding: Randomness](https://wiki.cavesofqud.com/wiki/Modding:Randomness)).
- **Unverified:** how road and river positions are matched across zone edges. The "mouth" builders suggest fixed, standard edge positions decided at world-map level, but I found no developer source. Also unverified: how zone seeds derive from zone IDs, and the developer talks on this (Bucklew's [Roguelike Celebration 2019 WFC talk](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1025914) covers WFC, not edge matching, as far as found).
- **Fit:** the two-level scheme fits if the coarse plan (which zone edges carry a road or river) is itself bounded-neighbourhood. Qud's world map is finite and global, so it doesn't need to be.

### Edge-matched region generation

- **Wang tiles** ([Cohen, Shade, Hiller, Deussen 2003](https://doi.org/10.1145/882262.882265)): square tiles with coloured edges, where adjacent edges must match. If each edge's colour is a hash of that edge's coordinates, every tile is chosen locally and neighbours agree without communicating.
- **Infinite Modifying in Blocks** ([Boris the Brave 2021](https://www.boristhebrave.com/2021/11/08/infinite-modifying-in-blocks/)): order-independent infinite WFC using 4 staggered block layers. Each final block needs 12 earlier blocks, about 4× the work, and patterns larger than a block are impossible. The same post notes that lazy chunked WFC is otherwise **not** deterministic across generation orders.
- **Hash-of-shared-edge ports** (no canonical primary source found; demonstrated in e.g. [problemecium's chunk demo](https://problemecium.itch.io/chunk-path-finding-demo), where "any two neighboring chunks' doors would match in location and facing direction"):
  1. Identify each region edge canonically, e.g. (west/north corner, axis), so both sides name it identically.
  2. Ports on an edge = `hash(seed, salt, edge)` → count and offsets, clear of the corners. Validate ports only against coarser, global-pure fields (water, cliffs) that both sides can evaluate. Take the first valid of _n_ hashed candidates.
  3. Inside the region, the generator must connect all of its ports, for example by routing them and the region's landmark first and filling obstacles afterwards. Then routes cannot fail.
  4. **Connectivity argument:** if every region connects all its ports and every shared edge between two participating regions has ≥ 1 port, the participating regions form a connected grid graph. The whole is then connected. Allowing 0-port edges breaks this unless a spanning structure is also chosen locally. This repo's `ownsLink` (each link owned by one side) is the same "decide once, both sides agree" idea.

**Common rules for us.** A space's ID must be computable from any entrance with bounded work: `hash(seed, kind, anchor)`, where the anchor is a hashed point owned by one macro cell (as camps and glades already are), never a flood-filled footprint. Entrances map to `(spaceId, portIndex)`, and the interior generator places port _i_ deterministically. Peers then agree on both sides, and only diffs and positions (which now include a space ID) cross the network.

## 4. Deep forest: three options

The aim: dense forest that players cross by paths. Current approach: [docs/worldgen.md](../worldgen.md#pipeline). Its trails are routed per macro cell on a 4-tile grid with an A\* expansion cap (`src/worldgen/routing.ts`), so a route can fail. The reuse discount only sees roads and trails of the same cell (`src/worldgen/world.ts`).

| Criterion                           | (a) In-place noise + routed trails (current)                                                                 | (b) In-place bespoke per macro cell, edge-hashed ports                                                                                                                                                       | (c) Separate space behind boundary entrances                                                                                                                           |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Continuity / feel                   | Seamless; density limited by noise, so it reads as "trees", not as "a forest you must follow paths through". | Seamless, overworld coordinates kept; interior can be fully authored (walls of trunks, glades, set pieces). The cell grid may show through unless port positions vary.                                       | Discontinuous: a loading-style transition. Free to be non-Euclidean or larger inside (Lost Woods).                                                                     |
| Guaranteed connectivity             | No: A\* can fail (expansion cap, water), and dense obstacles placed by noise can cut trails' surroundings.   | Yes, by construction: ports on every forest–forest edge, routes carved before obstacles are filled (§2).                                                                                                     | Yes within the space (one generator); the overworld side is just "go around".                                                                                          |
| Implementation                      | Exists.                                                                                                      | Moderate: an edge-port function, a per-cell forest generator, and rules at forest boundaries. Reuses `route`.                                                                                                | High: space model, entrance placement on a footprint, interior generator, transitions, and space-aware rendering, HUD and network.                                     |
| Chunk-locality / determinism        | Yes (bounded cells, hashed).                                                                                 | Yes: a cell depends on its own fields plus four hashed edges. The same shape as Minecraft structures.                                                                                                        | Yes if the footprint is defined by a bounded anchor. A footprint taken as a noise level set is unbounded and not locally computable, so it has to be clipped to cells. |
| Multiplayer                         | Nothing new.                                                                                                 | Nothing new.                                                                                                                                                                                                 | Positions gain a space ID. Peers in different spaces need interest management. Players in the forest are invisible from outside.                                       |
| Rivers / roads / mountains crossing | Natural: they're earlier stages, and trails discount roads.                                                  | Natural: they come from coarser stages as fixed constraints. A road crossing a cell edge is a port; rivers stay where they are and get bridges or fords. Mountains are kept, since height isn't regenerated. | Awkward: whatever crosses the footprint must be cut at the boundary or re-created inside, and the footprint shows as a hole or wall on the map.                        |

### Recommendation

**Recommendation:** choose (b), built as an evolution of (a), not a replacement. Make "deep forest" a band of the moisture field, as Minecraft does with Dark Forest. In a deep-forest macro cell:

1. take ports from a hash of each shared edge with another deep-forest cell, and add exits wherever a road or trail crosses into open land;
2. route trails from the ports to the cell's glade with the existing `route`, on a cost field that can't fail (no water veto; bridges as for roads);
3. only then fill with impassable trees except on ways and glades.

That gives guaranteed crossings, keeps rivers, roads, height and overworld coordinates, and needs nothing new from multiplayer. Keep (c), separate spaces, for what [ideas.md](../ideas.md#spaces-entrances-to-separately-generated-maps) already intends (caves, dungeons, interiors). If a "Lost Woods" feeling is wanted, that is a separate space with looping exits, not the overworld forest.

- Minecraft spline evaluation formula and extrapolation; the multi-noise distance metric; the `structure_references` radius of 8 chunks; whether the spline values equal those of 1.18.
- The temperature column of Dark Forest and Forest in the middle-biome table.
- Content of Kniberg's talks ([Reinventing Minecraft world generation, Jfokus](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ob3VwY4JyzE); [Minecraft terrain generation in a nutshell](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CSa5O6knuwI)): titles confirmed, transcripts not read.
