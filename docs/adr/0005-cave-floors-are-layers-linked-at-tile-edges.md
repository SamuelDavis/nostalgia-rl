# Cave floors are layers, linked at tile edges and generated one at a time

Underground, the world is a stack of floors per cave. A floor is a layer of tiles at the same x, y as the surface, with real heights that may slope and go below sea level or level 0. The surface is one more layer. A player is always on exactly one layer and only changes layer by stepping across a link: a pair of adjacent tiles on two layers at matching heights. A cave mouth links the surface to a floor, a ramp links one floor to the next, and a junction links floors of two neighbouring caves. Off a link, a step stays on the current layer, so the surface over a cave is untouched and walkable.

Each floor is generated by itself from its cave's plan: the anchor, territory, mouth, floor range, and points hashed per floor and per cave pair where ramps and junctions meet. Neighbouring floors agree on a ramp without reading each other's layout, and a junction between two caves is decided by one of them, as with way links ([ADR 0004](0004-ways-and-rivers-on-a-node-lattice.md)). Floors are spaced 8 m apart and slope only a little, and each floor keeps out of the column under or over its neighbours' ramps, so wherever two floors share an x, y they are at least 6 m apart.

We considered numbered levels with a single entrance (heights unrelated to the mountain), a 3D density volume (more memory per tile and a different renderer, for overhangs we don't need), and generating each cave whole (a deep cave would cost every floor to show one).

## Consequences

- Terrain lookups, chunk caches and player positions carry a layer.
- Floors above the surrounding ground stay under the mountain with headroom; floors below it fill the cave's territory. Height above ground follows the mountain; depth is the cave's own choice.
- Floor heights need a signed type wider than the surface's.
- Caves never overlap: they split a mountain into territories and meet only at junctions.
